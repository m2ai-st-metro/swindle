Write a Gumroad product description for a free developer tool. The reader is a
developer scrolling a storefront who has about 10 seconds to decide whether
this is worth downloading. Win that 10 seconds.

STRICT SOURCING RULES (read before writing anything)

1. Do NOT invent time savings, productivity gains, or percentage improvements.
   Phrases like "save 2+ hours/week", "cuts setup by 80%", "10x faster" are
   BANNED unless the exact claim appears in the Spec below. If it's not in
   the spec, it does not go in the listing.
2. Do NOT invent benchmark numbers, test counts, user counts, star counts,
   download counts, uptime percentages, or any other quantified metric. Zero
   fabricated numbers. If the spec does not state it, omit it.
3. Do NOT use superlatives ("most", "fastest", "best", "only", "unparalleled",
   "world-class", "industry-leading") unless the exact superlative is sourced
   from the spec. Default to neutral descriptive language.
4. Features and benefits must be derivable from the Spec or Source files
   content. No extrapolation, no "users typically see...", no "this means
   you'll be able to..." unless the spec says it directly.
5. If a claim you want to make is not supported by the spec, OMIT it. Do not
   soften it, paraphrase it, or hedge it ("may help you...", "could save...").
   Omission beats invention.
6. Tone: outcome-oriented but grounded. Describe what the repo DOES and WHO
   it's for. Do not claim what the buyer WILL ACHIEVE unless that outcome is
   explicitly promised in the spec.

When in doubt, describe the tool. Do not sell an outcome you cannot source.

Voice and shape

- Plain speak. Write how you'd describe this to a dev friend at a bar, not
  how you'd describe it in a quarterly report.
- Lead with what the tool does or who it's for. First sentence should make
  the reader recognize the problem space.
- Short paragraphs. 2-3 sentences each, max. White space is a feature.
- No headers unless they genuinely help. Aim for at most ONE short bullet
  list if you need to enumerate concrete things you get.
- Specific over generic, but only when specifics are sourced. "Python 3.11+,
  no external services" (sourced from spec) beats "streamlines your workflow"
  (vague). Never fabricate specifics to sound concrete.
- No hype words: revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, unleash,
  supercharge, next-generation, best-in-class, seamless. Cut them all.
- Honest about scope. "Does X, does not do Y" builds trust.
- Free. Don't belabor the price, it's free, just move on.
- Target length: 180-300 words. Shorter is better than longer.

Shape to follow

1. Opening paragraph (2-3 sentences): what the tool is and the problem space
   it sits in. Hook without hype.
2. One paragraph on what it actually does. Concrete, derived from the spec.
3. Optional: a short bullet list (3-5 items) of what you get IF enumerating
   things adds value. Every bullet must be sourced from the spec or files.
4. One paragraph on how to use it or what to expect. Setup basics from the
   spec. Only include friction notes if the spec mentions them.
5. Close with one sentence pointing to the repo or next step.

After the description, on separate lines:

SUMMARY: <one sentence, max 140 chars, the elevator pitch — this is the
Gumroad summary field, it shows up in search results, make it count.
Same sourcing rules apply: no invented numbers, no superlatives.>
TAGS: <3-5 comma-separated tags, lowercase, hyphens-not-spaces>

Do not include headers like "Description:" or "Output:". Do not explain
what you're about to do. Just write the description, then SUMMARY, then TAGS.
